# Claude self-analysis notes

A Claude self-analysis of the collected passages independently described several patterns that overlap with Own Words' corpus-derived rhetorical grammar:

- contrarian/corrective openings ("X isn't what you think; it's Y")
- repeated ranking language around the "real," "core," or most important factor
- mechanism-over-description explanations
- concession followed by a narrower or more defensible claim
- short aphoristic conclusions
- fragments, repetition, and lists of three or four
- heavy em-dash and colon usage
- authority framed through observation rather than credentials
- a recurring sequence of contrarian setup → mechanism → concession → compressed conclusion
- a broader "deflation" move that replaces a flashy or inflated framing with a modest, process-oriented one

These observations are useful as cross-validation of the grammar, not as ground truth about model identity. They should not be treated as a Claude detector or authorship signature. The most actionable product implication is to expose the rhetorical families touched by a rewrite alongside token-level Rewrite Depth metrics, and to continue preferring diverse discourse moves rather than repeated variants of the same construction.
